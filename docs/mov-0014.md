# MOV-0014: sampler callback index or float32 lag, and the delayed timer start

[Issue #20](https://github.com/galitianu/warcraft-re/issues/20), following
[MOV-0013](mov-0013.md#next-experiments).

**Reference.** WC3 1.29.2 (9231), x86 32-bit, `wc3-1.29.2-9231-x86`, on the selected
Windows 11 Pro ARM64 VM.

**Questions.**

1. Which callbacks read the 5 ms frame after the one rule H0 selects? In every
   retained 1 ms run, and in MOV-0013-a/c, they are boundary callbacks 15–35. There
   the sampler's own callback index (H1) and the float32 lag before the frame (3–7
   ulps) coincide.
2. Does a delayed library clock start on a 2⁻¹⁷ s grid? MOV-0013-b/c fitted that
   after the fact.

## Design

Three [scenario v2](scenario-v2.md) files in [scenarios/MOV-0014](../scenarios/MOV-0014/).
Each is MOV-0013-c with a different `observe.start`: one Footman at (0, 0) facing 0,
moved to (1000, 0) at callback 0. Sampling is 1 ms for 1000 callbacks, with the
oracle-v1 1000 s library clock and the 10 s STATE clock. The schema, generator and
library are unchanged.

| Map | `start` | Callback 0 | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| MOV-0014-a | 0.3 | 5 ulps (2⁻²⁴) after frame 60 | Same binade as MOV-0013; boundary lags 5 ulps smaller |
| MOV-0014-b | 0.72 | 6 ulps (2⁻²³) after frame 144 | Past 1 s, where the ulp doubles and lags grow 3 ulps per frame |
| MOV-0014-c | 0.9 | 7 ulps (2⁻²³) after frame 180 | As b; the two lag rules pick disjoint callbacks |

In all three, the sampler and the order start on a frame ≡ 0 (mod 6). The first
movement update after the order is therefore 10 ms later, as in MOV-0013-a. The first
moved frame, n₀ + 3, falls at boundary callback 15.

## Hypotheses

The lag is the time from callback k (H0 frame n, `c(n) ≤ s(k) < c(n+1)`) to frame n + 1.

| Frame rule | Callback k reads frame n + 1 when |
|---|---|
| **H0** | never |
| **H1** | k ∈ {15, 20, 25, 30, 35} and the lag is below 2⁻¹⁶ s |
| **Lu** | the lag is 3–7 ulps of the float32 time `s(k)` |
| **Ls** | the lag is 3–7 × 2⁻²⁴ s |

H1, Lu and Ls each reproduce MOV-0013 a–d exactly, and each predicts 15–35 for every
retained 1 ms run. None of them moves a callback in any retained 10, 20, 25 or 50 ms
schedule ([predictions.json](../results/mov-0014/predictions.json), `retained_check`).
Below 1 s, Lu and Ls are the same rule.

The library-clock rules give TICK = `TimerGetElapsed` of the 1000 s timer started
with the sampler at `s(0) = add(0.5, start)`:

| Clock rule | Reading |
|---|---|
| **M0** | `sub(1000, sub(add(s(0), 1000), s(k)))`, as MOV-0013 pre-registered; footman-v4's runner |
| **M1** | `sub(1000, ⌊s(0) + 1000 − s(k)⌋)`: exact remaining time, rounded down to float32 |
| **Q16/Q17/Q18** | M1 with the start `s(0)` rounded down to 2⁻¹⁶/2⁻¹⁷/2⁻¹⁸ s |
| **Qrel** | Q17 below 1 s, Q16 from 1 s: a quantum of 2⁷ ulps of `s(0)` |

Against the 2,002 MOV-0013 b/c TICKs, only Q17 and Qrel predict every one.

**Other expectations.** Movement onset is frame n₀ + 3 (on the update grid), and

frame n or n + 1; anything else is reported as observed.

## Decision rules (fixed before capture)

- A frame rule survives only if, in all three maps, its list equals the observed set
  of callbacks whose STATE `x_9`/`y_9` equal frame n + 1, and no callback reads any
  other frame. A clock rule survives only if it predicts all 1001 TICKs of every map.
- If **exactly one** of Lu or Ls survives, the v2 runner adopts it. It must still
  reproduce every retained capture; the before/after hashes are retained, and gated
  lists shrink only where the rule now predicts WC3.
- If H1 alone survives, nothing is implemented. The effect is then tied to the
  callback index, with no mechanism.
- If exactly one clock rule survives, the v2 runner's library clock adopts it under
  the same conditions.
- In every other case, or if no rule survives, the result is reported and gated as
  observed. No rule is fitted after capture.

## Results

**Clock: Q17 confirmed.** Q17 predicted all 3,003 TICKs. Every other rule missed some:

| Map | M0 | M1 | Q16 | Q17 | Q18 | Qrel |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| a | 126 | 71 | 126 | **0** | 61 | 0 |
| b | 375 | 110 | 125 | **0** | 63 | 125 |
| c | 500 | 102 | 0 | **0** | 62 | 0 |

Map b rules out Qrel and map a rules out Q16. Q17 also reproduces every TICK of the
31 earlier captures this parser reads. That covers 1–50 ms sampling, the bootstrap start
and MOV-0013's delayed starts; at the 0.5 s bootstrap Q17 equals M0. Phase 0's MOV-0002
predates the format, and MOV-0002-r2 reproduces it. The 10 s `c10_9`
clock matched its prediction at every callback. As pre-registered, the v2 runner now
uses Q17 ([Cases.cpp](../tools/wc3test/Cases.cpp), exact integer arithmetic).

**Frames: no rule survives.** Callbacks whose X/Y show frame n + 1, with the lag before
that frame in units of 2⁻²⁴ s:

| Map | Observed | Lag | H1 | Lu | Ls |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| a | 30–60 | 1–7 | 30, 35 ✗ | 40–60 ✗ | 40–60 ✗ |
| b | 15 | 6 | 15–35 ✗ | 15, 20 ✗ | 15 ✓ |
| c | 15 | 4 | 15–35 ✗ | 20 ✗ | 15 ✓ |

- **H1 is refuted.** The exceptions do not follow the sampler's callback index.
- **Lu is refuted.** At b and c's callback 20, lags of 6 and 5 ulps of 2⁻²³ read the
  H0 frame.
- **Ls predicted b and c exactly.** It failed only by its lower bound: a's callbacks 30
  and 35, at lags of 1 and 2 × 2⁻²⁴ s, also read the next frame.
- **Onset** was frame n₀ + 3 in all three maps, as predicted. Every callback read
  frame n or n + 1.

**Candidate (post hoc, not implemented).** Every observation in MOV-0013 and MOV-0014
fits one statement. A callback less than 8 × 2⁻²⁴ s (4.8e-7 s) before a frame shows
that frame's X/Y; at 8 or more (MOV-0013's callback 40; b and c's callback 20 at 12
and 10) it does not. This is a lag in seconds, not in ulps of the time and not
callback-relative. Facing at an update frame behaves differently:

- At a's callback 40 (lag 3, an update frame), WC3 printed the next frame's X/Y but
  the facing from before the update.
- MOV-0013-a's callback 10 (lag 2) also printed the pre-update facing.

That is the same split as the unexplained 10/25 ms anomalies
([footman-v4 results](footman-v4-results.md#sampling-order)). The candidate was fitted
after this capture, so the decision rules leave the frame selection at H0.

**C++ comparison.** The same scenarios run through `wc3test` (footman-v4, H0, Q17),
twice with identical bytes. Under the unchanged tolerances, all three fail only on x/y,
at exactly the callbacks above; the clock, facing, order and STATE c10 match WC3 at
every callback. The first divergences are x at a's callback 30 (5.399414 against 4.04980469) and at
b and c's callback 15 (1.349609 against 0). [run_mov0013.py](../tools/run_mov0013.py)
requires exactly these differences ([report](../results/mov-0014/mov-0014.md)).

**Runner change.** Every MOV-0001–0010 model/phase payload, full trace and CSV is
unchanged. For MOV-0013 v2, a and d are byte-identical; b and c change only their
reported times (875 and 624 TICKs), and now match WC3's clock exactly
([runner-before-after.json](../results/mov-0014/runner-before-after.json)). The
MOV-0013 gate's clock lists shrank to none, and MOV-0013-b now passes canonically.

## Next experiment

Test the post-hoc lag candidate before adopting it, with its predictions written first:

1. Starts whose boundary lags straddle 7–9 × 2⁻²⁴ s with a moving unit.
2. A boundary callback 1–2 × 2⁻²⁴ s before an update frame, to separate X/Y from facing.

## Update from MOV-0015

[MOV-0015](mov-0015.md) tested the candidate with predictions written first. The lag
rule (T7) predicted three of four new maps exactly, including one past 2 s; a
float32-offset alternative was refuted. The fourth map's clocks show its sampler started
one ulp later than the runner assumes, and from that start T7 also fits it. A separate
30 ms update clock (FU) predicted the facings past 2 s that the frame clock missed. The
start rounding was found after capture, so the runner still uses H0 until a follow-up
confirms all three together.
