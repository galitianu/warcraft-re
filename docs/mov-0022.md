# MOV-0022: the retail pathing mechanism on new layouts

[Issue #27](https://github.com/galitianu/warcraft-re/issues/27). **Priority: P3, obvious
in play** (AGENTS.md). Follows the [retail pathing mechanism](footman-v4-model.md#unit-pathing).

**Reference.** WC3 1.29.2 (9231), x86 32-bit, `wc3-1.29.2-9231-x86`, on the selected
Windows 11 Pro ARM64 VM.

**Question.** footman-v4's pathing was read from the retail executable. It reproduces
all 26 MOV-0018–0021 captures exactly, but those captures guided the analysis. Two of its
rules were also chosen to fit them:

- the most recently ordered unit updates first;
- units with a nonzero velocity are not obstacles to planning or to the straight-line test.

Does the mechanism predict layouts it has not seen? This experiment tests unequal speeds
(the squared-speed comparison), the order of issue, a slow crossing unit, a diagonal past
an idle unit (A* tie order and waypoint skipping), and an overtake.

## Design (fixed before capture)

Six [scenario v1](scenario-v1.md) files in [scenarios/MOV-0022](../scenarios/MOV-0022/),
in MOV-0019's shape: two `hfoo` of Player(0), 20 ms sampling, 600 callbacks, orders at
callback 0 in the listed order, each unit facing its target. Speeds are requested with
`move_speed`; the default is 270.

| Variant | u0 | u1 | Orders | Tests |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a | (0, 0) → (1000, 0), speed 300 | (1000, 0) → (0, 0) | u0, u1 | the faster unit goes and the slower is held |
| b | (0, 0) → (1000, 0) | (1000, 0) → (0, 0), speed 300 | u0, u1 | a with the speeds swapped |
| c | as a | as a | u1, u0 | processing order |
| d | (0, 0) → (1000, 0) | (500, −380) → (500, 620), speed 200 | u0, u1 | a slower crossing unit |
| e | (0, 0) → (1000, 1000) | idle at (500, 500) | u0 | A* on a diagonal, waypoint skipping |
| f | (0, 0) → (1200, 0), speed 400 | (200, 0) → (1000, 0), speed 150 | u0, u1 | an overtake: planning ignores the moving unit |

Each map is captured in three fresh WC3 processes with the Phase 7 command.

[tools/predict_mov0022.py](../tools/predict_mov0022.py) wrote the scenarios and
[predictions.json](../results/mov-0022/predictions.json). Each prediction is the complete
footman-v4 trace, built from `2ab1e5c` (clean), stored in
[predicted/](../results/mov-0022/predicted/) with its digest. Summary, in callbacks
(20 ms):

| Variant | u0 waits | u1 waits | u0 side, arrival | u1 side, arrival |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a | 82–84 | 83–113 | left, 182 | left, 217 |
| b | 83–113 | 83–86 | right, 214 | right, 176 |
| c | 82–84 | 82–111 | left, 179 | left, 214 |
| d | 80–83 | 82–111 | left, 188 | left, 278 |
| e | none | idle | left, 265 (deviates 89 units) | — |
| f | 26–29, 31–44, 47–50 | 28–57, 70–77 | right, 175 | left, 301 |

In a, b and d the slower unit is held for about 20 updates, and the faster one waits
briefly, then routes around it. a and c differ only in timing (one update).

### Decision rules (before capture)

- **Pass:** a variant whose capture is canonical PASS against its predicted trace.
- **P1 pass:** comparator differences that are all P1 (sub-unit, sub-degree, sub-frame).
  They are documented, gated with their counts and bounds, and not pursued.
- **Fail:** a P2 or P3 difference. The first divergence names the rule to revisit:
  - a and b: the speed comparison;
  - c: the processing order;
  - d and f: holding and replanning;
  - e: the A* tie order or waypoint skipping;
  - f also tests whether planning ignores moving units.

  No rule is refitted without a new pre-registered experiment.

## Results

**Captures.** All six maps were captured on the verified installation
(`oracle/environment/mov-0022-*-verification.json`). Each ran in three fresh WC3
processes, with identical payloads every time. The fixtures are
`oracle/canonical-generated/wc3-1.29.2-9231-x86/MOV-0022-*.json`. The WATCH records show
the requested speeds applied (300, 200, 400 and 150), so the speed setters on several
units ran as declared.

e was captured first, by the generator before speed setters on several units were
allowed. Its case JASS is byte-identical under the current generator, and its retained
generation record keeps the earlier generator's source digest.

**Grading** ([run_mov0018.py](../tools/run_mov0018.py), [report](../results/mov-0022/grading.md)):
every variant is **canonical PASS** against its pre-registered trace. The C++ trace in
each capture bundle is byte-identical to the stored prediction.

| Variant | Canonical | Sides | Arrival WC3 / C++ | Rule tested |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a | PASS | left, left | 182/182, 217/217 | the speed comparison |
| b | PASS | right, right | 214/214, 176/176 | the speed comparison |
| c | PASS | left, left | 179/179, 214/214 | the processing order |
| d | PASS | left, left | 188/188, 278/278 | holding and replanning |
| e | PASS | left | 265/265 | the A* tie order, waypoint skipping |
| f | PASS | right, left | 175/175, 301/301 | planning ignores moving units |

The largest comparator difference is 5×10⁻⁶ (f, u0's x). It is decimal printing only:
WC3 prints six decimals, C++ the shortest round-trip text, and both name the same
float32. There is no P3, P2 or P1 difference.

**Decision.** Pass, under the rules fixed before capture. The two interpreted rules
(the most recently ordered unit updates first; moving units are not obstacles) predicted
layouts they were not chosen for.
