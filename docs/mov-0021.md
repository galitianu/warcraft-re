# MOV-0021: who waits head-on with a lateral offset

[Issue #27](https://github.com/galitianu/warcraft-re/issues/27). **Priority: P3, obvious
in play** (AGENTS.md). Follows [MOV-0020](mov-0020.md#post-hoc-refit-giving-way).

**Reference.** WC3 1.29.2 (9231), x86 32-bit, `wc3-1.29.2-9231-x86`, on the selected
Windows 11 Pro ARM64 VM.

**Question.** Two Footmen meet head-on, their travel lines 32 units apart (MOV-0019-j,
MOV-0020-b, d). In WC3 the eastward unit waits about 20 updates (0.6 s), and the westward unit
steps past it. That holds whichever unit was created or ordered first. footman-v4 does
not reproduce it (P3): each unit sees the other on its left, so neither gives way. What
picks the unit that waits?

The layout is symmetric under a half turn, up to the 32-unit pathing grid, so a rule
based on the units' relative positions alone cannot pick a unit. Something absolute
must break the tie. The unoffset MOV-0018-b constrains it: there the *westward* unit
waits 10 updates and the eastward one routes around after 2. So travel direction alone
does not decide every head-on.

Three hypotheses name the waiting unit, and each fits MOV-0019-j and MOV-0020-b, d:

- **heading:** the unit whose heading is the smaller angle anticlockwise from east
  (east 0°, north 90°, west 180°, south 270°). MOV-0018-b already contradicts it
  without an offset, so it can only hold for offset head-ons.
- **rotation:** the outcome turns and mirrors with the layout. The image of j's eastward
  unit waits. A half turn of j equals j shifted by (−1000, −32) with the roles swapped.
  This hypothesis therefore implies that the layout's position on the grid matters.
- **line:** the unit on the lower travel line waits: lower y for east–west travel,
  lower x for north–south. Undefined for MOV-0018-b, where both share one line.

## Design (fixed before capture)

Six [scenario v1](scenario-v1.md) files in [scenarios/MOV-0021](../scenarios/MOV-0021/),
in MOV-0019's shape: two `hfoo` of Player(0), 20 ms sampling, 600 callbacks, both
orders at callback 0, u0 first, each unit facing its target. u0 is always the image of
j's eastward unit.

| Variant | u0 | u1 | Waits under heading / rotation / line |
|---|---|---|---|
| a | (0, 0) → (1000, 0) | (1000, −32) → (0, −32) | u0 / u0 / u1 |
| b | (0, 0) → (0, 1000) | (−32, 1000) → (−32, 0) | u0 / u0 / u1 |
| c | (0, 0) → (0, −1000) | (32, −1000) → (32, 0) | u1 / u0 / u0 |
| d | (0, 0) → (0, 1000) | (32, 1000) → (32, 0) | u0 / u0 / u0 |
| e | (0, 0) → (1000, 0) | (1100, 32) → (100, 32) | u0 / u0 / u0 |
| f | (0, 0) → (1000, 0) | (1100, −32) → (100, −32) | u0 / u0 / u1 |

- a is j mirrored, so the offset falls on each unit's right.
- b and c are j turned 90° anticlockwise and clockwise. d is b mirrored.
- e and f remove a confound. In j each unit's target is covered by the other unit when
  its order is issued, so the second unit takes a partial route that bends it off its
  line before the meeting. In e and f no target is covered, and both units walk
  straight lines 32 units apart. If e's eastward unit waits as in j, the partial route
  is not what decides.

Every pair of hypotheses names different units in at least one variant.

Each map is captured in three fresh WC3 processes with the Phase 7 command.

### Decision rules (before capture)

1. **Who waits.** As in MOV-0020, a unit waits if it has a wait of at least 7 callbacks
   (5 updates). A hypothesis survives if it names exactly the waiting unit in all six
   variants. A variant with no such wait, or with both units waiting, contradicts every
   hypothesis and is reported as such.
2. **Partial routes.** If e's waiting unit differs from j's (the eastward unit), the
   partial route is part of what decides. Then e and f are graded separately from a–d.
3. **Next step.**
   - If one hypothesis survives, footman-v4 breaks offset head-on ties by it. The fit is
     reported as post hoc and graded at P3 against MOV-0018 to MOV-0021.
   - If none survives, the observed waiters are reported with their ratings, and the
     next experiment is recorded.

Out of scope: crossings, other offsets, terrain and doodad pathing, other unit types,
more than two units, different owners, groups and formations.

## Observations (post hoc)

> **Superseded.** footman-v4 now follows the retail pathing mechanism read from the game
> code ([footman-v4-model.md](footman-v4-model.md#unit-pathing)). It reproduces all 26
> MOV-0018–0021 captures exactly. The fitted model below is kept as history.

These are read from the captures after grading. They are not a fitted rule.

- **Turning the layout changes the outcome.** c is j turned clockwise, and b is j
  turned anticlockwise. Their positions relative to each other are the same as j's
  throughout. Yet in j u0 holds 20 updates and u1 one; in c u0 holds 2 and u1 5; in b
  neither unit stops at all. So the decision depends on absolute position or direction,
  not only on the units' relative geometry.
- **Mirroring keeps the waiting role but not the response.** In a (j mirrored), the
  eastward unit still waits, but only 10 updates, and the westward one swings 47.7 units
  wide instead of j's 26.9.
- **a against b.** These two have the same relative geometry (u1 32 units to u0's
  right), one travelling east–west and the other north–south. In a, the eastward unit's
  y drifts from +0.0005 to −0.024 just before the units meet (callbacks 84–86), crossing
  a 32-unit cell boundary. In b, the northward unit's x stays exactly 0. In a both units
  stop at k57/k58; in b neither stops. A footprint made of grid cells, tested against
  the unit's own cell, would make such a drift matter. But a quick search of
  cell-overlap stop rules over all 26 two-Footman captures found none that fits the
  stop updates. In particular, a and j share u0's exact trajectory, but their u1s sit
  on opposite sides of it, and no single cell-difference threshold makes both stop.
- **The response comes in a few fixed lengths.** Almost every hold lasts 1, 2, 5, 10 or 20 updates (one in e lasts 9).

**Status** (P3 acceptance and ratings, gated in [run_mov0018.py](../tools/run_mov0018.py)):

| Variant | Difference from WC3 | Rating |
|---|---|---|
| a | u0 waits about 2 updates instead of 10 and arrives 9 callbacks early; u1 swings 15.6 wide instead of 47.7 | **P3** |
| b | the model stops u1 for about 5 updates where WC3 does not stop it, arriving 7 callbacks late | P2 |
| c, d | the model misses a 2-update hold and a 5-update wait; arrivals 6–8 callbacks off; finals within 3 units | P2 |

With MOV-0019-j and MOV-0020-b, d, the head-on offset family has five P3 variants. The
issue's acceptance is still **not met**.

## Next experiment

The sub-cell sweep planned here is no longer needed. The retail mechanism explains the
orientation dependence: a cell footprint anchored at `floor(position)`, a speed
comparison decided by soft-float ulps, and the most recently ordered unit updating first.
It reproduces all six variants exactly ([model](footman-v4-model.md#unit-pathing)).
MOV-0022 tests that mechanism on layouts it has not seen.
