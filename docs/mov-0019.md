# MOV-0019: pinning the unit-pathing fits

[Issue #27](https://github.com/galitianu/warcraft-re/issues/27). **Priority: P3, obvious
in play** (AGENTS.md). Follows [MOV-0018](mov-0018.md).

**Reference.** WC3 1.29.2 (9231), x86 32-bit, `wc3-1.29.2-9231-x86`, on the selected
Windows 11 Pro ARM64 VM.

**Question.** footman-v4's unit pathing was fitted to four captures. Do its footprint,
tie order and wait corridor hold in layouts it was not fitted to? Three parts rest on
little evidence:

- **Footprint.** Another unit blocks the 3×3 cells around its cell. This was tested only
  with blockers at y = 0 and 24, which lie in the same cell row.
- **Tie order.** When routes on both sides of an obstacle are equally long, the unit
  passes south. This was seen only once, travelling east.
- **Wait corridor.** Many combinations of `block_radius`, `block_lookahead`, `block_lateral`
  and `recheck_updates` fit MOV-0018's waits equally well.

If any of these is wrong, a player would see the wrong side or a missing or extra wait
in other layouts. Those are P3 errors.

at callback 0. u0 starts facing its target.

### Decision rules (before capture)

1. **Footprint.** If a–d match the model's P3 description, the 3×3 footprint is
   confirmed for these rows. If c differs from b at P3, cell quantisation is refuted.
   If any of a–d differs, the footprint is refitted to all fourteen captures and the
   fit is reported as post hoc.
2. **Tie.** The table above names the rule that e and f support. A rule contradicted by
   either variant is rejected.
3. **Corridor.** The candidates that survive are those whose predicted outcome equals
   WC3's P3 description in all ten variants.
   - If the adopted set survives, it stays.
   - If others survive but it does not, adopt the survivor with the smallest
     |Δradius| + |Δlookahead| + |Δlateral| from (62, 32, 40). Record that as a post-hoc
     choice among pre-registered candidates.
   - If none survives, the corridor rule is refuted. Report the differences at their
     ratings and record the next experiment.
4. **Acceptance** (issue #27):
   - Every MOV-0018 and MOV-0019 variant matches WC3 at P3 level: same side,
     waits within 2 callbacks, arrival within 2 callbacks, finals within 1 unit and
     the same orders.
   - Remaining differences are rated and gated as in [run_mov0018.py](../tools/run_mov0018.py).
   - MOV-0001–0010 and the MOV-0013/14/15/18 gates do not regress, and the
     five-configuration CI passes.

Out of scope: terrain and doodad pathing, other unit types, more than two units,
groups and formations.

## Post-hoc analysis and refit

Everything below was fitted after seeing the MOV-0019 captures. It is a hypothesis
until a new pre-registered capture tests it.

**Partial routes.** From callback 1, j's u1 heads for (16, 80). That is the centre of
the free cell nearest its covered target (0, 32), not the last free node on the A* path,
(80, 48), which the model used. MOV-0018-b's u1 heads for (80, 16), one of four free
nodes equally near its target, and the nearest of them to the start. The model now
routes to the reachable free node nearest the target, breaking ties by path length
from the start. j's u0 is ordered while u1 still stands idle beside u0's target, and
it walks straight. An idle unit that covers the target node is now ignored, which
extends the MOV-0018-b rule (an idle unit standing on the target). With both
changes, j matches WC3 exactly up to callback 86. MOV-0018-b is unchanged.

**Line clearance.** Clearances in about (2.2, 2.8] make b, c and e exact. They keep
MOV-0018-a/d and MOV-0019-a/f exact, but at P3 MOV-0018-b gains an extra wait: its
u0 route passes nearer the other unit, and the refuted corridor fires. The clearance
stays at 4 until the corridor is known. At 4, b and c are within 8.6 units and e within
3.3 (P2).

**Who waits.** [onsets_mov0019.py](../tools/onsets_mov0019.py) finds, from the WC3
fixtures alone, the update at which each unit stopped. It reports where the other unit
was in that unit's frame ([onsets.json](../results/mov-0019/onsets.json)). "Along" is
distance ahead and "left" is lateral offset.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| MOV-0018-b | u0 | k57 | 2 | (79.3, 13.1), head-on | (95.6, 12.5) |
| MOV-0018-b | u1 | k57 | 10 | (79.3, 13.3), head-on | (95.5, 13.4) |

Waits last 10, 15 or 20 updates, consistent with the 5-update recheck. Every onset
except i's comes at about 80 units ahead (79.3–80.2), never at 87 or more. But no
region of (along, left) explains the onsets, whether or not it extrapolates the other
unit's velocity:

- c's u0 does not stop at (62.6, −62.7).
- i's u0 stops at (31.3, −71.7), with the other unit approaching at the same speed.
- j's u0 stops 59 units to the side of a unit that is moving away from its line.

The response also differs. In MOV-0018-b, u1 waits for 10 updates while u0 replans
around it. In i and j, u1 holds for one update, turns, and passes in front of the
waiting u0 (j's u1 via (496, 48), the first A* step).

The model processes units in index order. In it, the unit that is blocked first
waits, and a waiting unit replans when its blocker stops. Neither processing order
fits b, i and j together. In the five crossing and offset captures (c, g, h, i, j),
**u0** (created and ordered first) is the unit that waits, even in i, where it would
reach the crossing first. In the head-on b, both stop at k57, then u0 replans around
u1. So the lower-index or earlier-ordered unit may give way. That hypothesis and a
geometric one predict different units waiting when the two units' roles are swapped.
The current captures cannot tell them apart.
