# MOV-0020: who gives way when two Footmen cross

[Issue #27](https://github.com/galitianu/warcraft-re/issues/27). **Priority: P3, obvious
in play** (AGENTS.md). Follows [MOV-0019](mov-0019.md#post-hoc-analysis-and-refit).

**Reference.** WC3 1.29.2 (9231), x86 32-bit, `wc3-1.29.2-9231-x86`, on the selected
Windows 11 Pro ARM64 VM.

**Question.** In every crossing and offset head-on so far (MOV-0018-c, MOV-0019-g to j),
u0 is the unit that waits. u0 was created first, ordered first and travelled east. In
MOV-0019-i it waited even though it would have reached the crossing first, and u1
stepped around it. No geometric rule fitted the onsets. What decides which unit gives
way? Showing the wrong unit waiting is a P3 error.

Three hypotheses name the unit that waits:

- **index:** the lower-index unit, u0;
- **order:** the unit ordered first;
- **role:** the unit travelling east. That role waited in every earlier capture, so
  the outcome would follow from the geometry.

## Design (fixed before capture)

Six [scenario v1](scenario-v1.md) files in [scenarios/MOV-0020](../scenarios/MOV-0020/),
in MOV-0019's shape: two `hfoo` of Player(0), 20 ms sampling, 600 callbacks, both
orders at callback 0 in the listed order, each unit facing its target.

| Variant | u0 | u1 | Ordered first | Waits under index / order / role |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a | (500, −540) → (500, 460) | (0, 0) → (1000, 0) | u0 | u0 / u0 / u1 |
| b | (1000, 32) → (0, 32) | (0, 0) → (1000, 0) | u0 | u0 / u0 / u1 |
| c | (0, 0) → (1000, 0) | (500, −540) → (500, 460) | u1 | u0 / u1 / u0 |

| f | (500, −440) → (500, 560) | (0, 0) → (1000, 0) | u0 | u0 / u0 / u1 |

Their sources:

- a and c are MOV-0019-i, b and d are MOV-0019-j, e is MOV-0018-c and f is MOV-0019-g.
- a, b, e and f swap the units' indices and order of issue.

In e the crossing is symmetric, so the role hypothesis rests on the earlier
observation alone.

Each map is captured in three fresh WC3 processes with the Phase 7 command.

### Decision rules (before capture)

1. **Who gives way.** In each variant, the WC3 waiting unit is any unit with a wait of
   at least 7 callbacks (5 updates). A hypothesis survives if it names exactly that
   unit in all six variants. A variant with no such wait, or with both units waiting,
   contradicts every hypothesis, and is reported as such.
2. **Partial routes.** In b and d, WC3 and the model must agree within 1 unit in x and y
   for both units from callback 0 to 80, before any unit is near the other. Otherwise
   the post-hoc rule is refuted for that case.
3. **Next step.**
   - If one hypothesis survives, footman-v4 gives way by it. The trigger and the
     sidestep are refitted to MOV-0018, 0019 and 0020, reported as post hoc, and graded
     at P3 as in MOV-0019 rule 4.
   - If none survives, the differences are reported at their ratings and the next
     experiment is recorded.

Out of scope: terrain and doodad pathing, other unit types, more than two units,
different owners, groups and formations.

## Post-hoc refit: giving way

> **Superseded.** footman-v4 now follows the retail pathing mechanism read from the game
> code ([footman-v4-model.md](footman-v4-model.md#unit-pathing)). It reproduces all 26
> MOV-0018–0021 captures exactly. The fitted model below is kept as history.

Fitted after seeing these captures; it is a hypothesis until a new pre-registered
capture tests it.

"East" is not a rule a pathing engine would hold. In every crossing, the unit that keeps
going has the waiting unit on its **left** when it first sees it in its path, and the
waiting unit has it on its right. footman-v4 now does this:

- A unit blocked by a **moving unit on its left** that does not have this unit in its
  own path holds for one update. It then plans around that unit and goes on, and that
  unit holds for **20 updates** (`give_way_updates`) from its next update, as i's and
  a's waiting unit does.
- If that unit does have this one in its path, on its own right, this unit goes on and
  the other unit's own test makes it wait (MOV-0018-c, MOV-0020-e).
- Otherwise both wait as before, including head-on, where each unit sees the other on
  its left (MOV-0018-b).

The line clearance stays at 4: at 2.5, MOV-0019-b, c and e become exact, but MOV-0018-b's
u0 gains a wait and a different final position (P3).

**Head-on with an offset (P3, open).** In WC3 the eastward unit waits about 20 updates
(0.6 s) and the westward one passes it with a small sidestep (−4.6 to −27 units). In the
model, both units see the other on their left, so neither gives way: the eastward unit
waits only 2–4 updates or not at all, and the westward unit swings 39–47 units wide.
Arrival differs by up to 30 callbacks. At the stop (k57), each unit has the other about
79 units ahead and 59–62 units to its left in every such capture, so the geometry is
symmetric and does not say which unit yields. Only direction distinguishes them so far.

The gate ([run_mov0018.py](../tools/run_mov0018.py), run in CI for MOV-0018, 0019 and
0020) records each of these differences with its rating. It fails if any of them changes,
disappears or appears.

The issue's acceptance is **not met**: the head-on offset family is P3. **Next experiment
(MOV-0021):** the MOV-0019-j head-on rotated to north–south and mirrored (offset −32,
and the westward unit on the other side), to find whether travel direction or which
side the offset falls on decides who waits head-on.
